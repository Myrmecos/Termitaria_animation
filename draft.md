One agent can answer questions, analyze information, propose a plan, or draft a report (show agent icons and pop up text bubbles). But complex projects require many of these jobs at once (overlay the text bubbles into a row). Asking one agent to do everything in a single conversation makes the work harder to steer, inspect, verify, and build upon (pile the bubbles onto a single agent icon). (0-15s)

Termitaria gives a complex project a team (show the icons of team members for autoresearch). A user sets the direction from the surface (user icon, pop up a text bubble showing "research"); beneath it, specialized agents carry out distinct responsibilities (show agents talking, in teams channel style). Their work becomes shared project knowledge rather than disappearing into one long chat (animation showing text boxes merge into a small graph). (15-28s)

Complex work is rarely linear (show a linear progress: literature gap scan-> hypotehsis -> experiment design -> method review). Users may need to check a decision, clarify a direction, or ask a specialist a follow-up question without derailing the entire project (user icon, arrows cut into the linear progress, stemming arrows pointing back). In Termitaria, users can inspect the team’s work and speak directly with individual agents (show the conversation panel where user clicks into a specific agent's conversation and ask)—more like checking in with a specific teammate than interrupting a single all-purpose assistant. (28s-51s)

Projects also evolve over time. A market brief may need revision when a competitor moves, policy changes, or new evidence emerges. Termitaria separates what the team learns from what each specialist needs to remember: a shared knowledge graph preserves project-level evidence and relationships, while each agent maintains its own role-specific memory for efficient recall across sessions. (51s-1m15s)

Like a termite colony, no single member holds the whole plan. Specialized members contribute their own work, preserve what they learn, and coordinate to build something larger. That is why we call it **Termitaria** (1m15s-1m27s).
